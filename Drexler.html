Clyde Drexler

Why Clyde Drexler is one of the all time great Blazers

The argument for Clyde Drexler being one of the greatest players in Portland Trail Blazers history rests on his rare combination of sustained team success, two-way statistical dominance, and unmatched longevity with the franchise. Across his 11 full seasons in Portland, the Trail Blazers never missed the playoffs, highlighted by a franchise-record 63-win campaign in 1990–91 and two NBA Finals appearances in 1990 and 1992 where Drexler served as the team's undisputed engine. Unlike stars who excel in only one area, the 6'7" shooting guard dominated every facet of the game, remaining the Blazers' all-time leader in games played (867), steals (1,795), and field goals made (6,889), while ranking second in points (18,040) and rebounds (5,339), third in assists (4,933), and first in nearly every major franchise postseason category. His elite individual peak—marked by eight All-Star selections in Portland, an All-NBA First Team nod, a selection to the iconic 1992 Olympic "Dream Team," and a 1992 MVP runner-up finish behind only Michael Jordan—bridges the gap between Bill Walton’s brief championship peak and Damian Lillard’s modern scoring records, cementing Drexler's place on the Mount Rushmore of Trail Blazers basketball.
