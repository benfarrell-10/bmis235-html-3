Bill Walton

Why Bill Walton is one of the greatest Blazers ever

The argument for Bill Walton being the greatest player in Portland Trail Blazers history rests on the fact that he achieved the highest individual and team peak the franchise has ever seen. Although chronic foot injuries limited him to just 209 regular-season games across four seasons in Portland, Walton delivered the city's first and only NBA championship in 1977, capturing Finals MVP honors after anchoring the defense, leading the league in both rebounds (14.4 per game) and blocks (3.2 per game), and orchestrating Jack Ramsay’s unselfish motion offense as one of the greatest passing big men in basketball history. He followed that title run by winning the 1977–78 NBA regular-season MVP award—remaining the sole MVP in Trail Blazers history—while guiding Portland to a 48–10 record before a devastating foot injury derailed a potential dynasty. Where Clyde Drexler and Damian Lillard built their legacies on decade-long durability and cumulative franchise records, Walton’s case is defined by unmatched two-way perfection at his summit, sparking the original "Blazermania" and securing the ultimate prize that no other Blazer has ever brought back to Rip City.
